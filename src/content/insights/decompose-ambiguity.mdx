In my [billing recovery work at RealPage](/work/billing-recovery-and-reconciliation), exceptions included missing order matches, inconsistent customer or property identifiers, and product or bundle configuration problems.

I translated those discrepancies into field mappings, transformation rules and reconciliation requirements. The immediate need was to produce importable billing lines for failed transactions while the underlying corrections progressed.

An interim Alteryx process supported that work before month-end close. I defined the mappings, rules and validation requirements and coordinated with Revenue Operations. The data-warehouse and implementation teams built the model.

For a similar investigation, I would write an exception as a checkable requirement: identify the source and target fields, define the expected mapping, specify how rejected rows are handled, and state how the output will reconcile. Those details are more useful to the delivery team than a broad instruction to replace or automate the workflow.
