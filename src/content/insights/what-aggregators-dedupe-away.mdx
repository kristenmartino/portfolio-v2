[Sift](/work/sift) compares coverage using retrieved articles and model-generated claims. The useful question is specific: what did these sources say about this topic?

The companion MCP tool starts with Sift's article index. When coverage is sparse, it can add web-search results. Claims retain an `index` or `web` source tag, and index claims can carry article IDs for opening the underlying reporting. The [tool implementation](https://github.com/kristenmartino/sift-mcp/blob/main/src/sift_mcp/server.py) shows those paths.

A result therefore depends on which articles were retrieved, when they were available and which claims the model extracted. An agreement label describes the tool's comparison of that set. It does not establish a consensus across all reporting or verify the claim itself.

This is why the route back to the article matters. A reviewer may find a qualification, a later update or a difference between the article and the generated wording. Civic reference pages can supply background, but their coverage also varies; some entities and fields have no entry.

My next review task would be to choose one comparison claim, open its cited reporting and check whether the wording preserves the source's qualifications. I would record a missing citation and an unsupported interpretation as different failures, since they need different fixes.
