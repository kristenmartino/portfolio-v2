Grouping articles about the same event can make a news feed easier to scan. It can also hide useful differences if the group replaces every source with a single summary.

For [Sift](/work/sift), I wanted readers to compare coverage and open the original reporting. A shared event does not mean the articles make identical claims. One may include a qualification, a different source or a later update. A comparison should preserve those details and let the reader inspect them.

There is a second problem: readers may need context before they can compare coverage. Sift includes background explainers, term definitions and sourced reference pages about people, organizations and legislation mentioned in stories.

These are design choices, rather than evidence that reading more sources changes someone's view. To evaluate the product, I would test whether readers can locate the original claims, identify differences and find the context they need.
