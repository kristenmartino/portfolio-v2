In [SpecialtyPulse](/work/specialtypulse-pipeline), the procedure view answers a narrower question than the specialty summary: what was the average payment per service for a particular procedure?

For Dermatology / HCPCS 99213, the synthetic 2023 fixture contains **57 qualifying rows and 3,777 services**. Summing each row's payment multiplied by service count gives **$396,247.22**. Dividing by 3,777 gives **$104.91 per service**, rounded to cents. The [verification artifact](https://github.com/kristenmartino/specialtypulse_pipeline/blob/60ef4479fd197f7abf171907f9a93b757d6e5085/data/payment_verification.json) lists the inputs and source-file hash.

A simple average of row-level payments would give each row equal influence, even when one represents many more services. Weighting by service count answers the stated question. It still does not tell us whether a procedure is underpaid or whether a sales opportunity exists.

The displayed value now comes from the same local Spark mart as the verification output. That supports the path from fixture to calculation to screen. It does not establish that the synthetic comparison years describe historical Medicare trends, or that the external Databricks, Airflow and Domo services executed.

For a dashboard review, I would start with one displayed value: name the denominator, recover its source rows and reproduce the calculation. Then ask an intended user what decision that value supports. Reconciliation and usefulness are separate checks; a successful calculation does not answer both.
