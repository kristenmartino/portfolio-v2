## What I built and ran

I built SpecialtyPulse for an analyst reviewing specialty payment trends, procedure differences and sales exposure. A local Spark run now takes **29,982 raw records through shared ingestion, staging and mart transformations to 870 rows used by the live dashboard**. The Databricks notebooks call those same transformation functions.

The normal procedure list displays **$104.91 across 3,777 services** for Dermatology / HCPCS 99213. That value reconciles to the raw records, the exported Spark mart and the browser data. The [run receipt](https://github.com/kristenmartino/specialtypulse_pipeline/blob/main/data/pipeline_run.json) records execution time, runtime versions and source/code/output hashes.

All records are synthetic CMS-schema fixtures. The 2021–2022 inputs are explicitly generated comparison scenarios based on the synthetic 2023 fixture, rather than historical CMS observations. Sales and usage are synthetic too.

## Analytical decisions

**Weight payments by service count.** The question is average payment per billed service, so larger service volumes contribute more. For the reconciled example, a simple average across the 57 source rows is $103.80; weighting by their 3,777 services gives $104.91. The difference shows why the denominator matters.

**Keep procedure comparisons specific.** I rank the most-used procedures by service count and display payment alongside them. A specialty's blended average combines different procedures; a payment below that blend is not evidence that a particular procedure is underpaid. The outlier flag therefore has its own ratio-based definition, available beside the table.

**Keep scores and sales dollars separate.** The pressure index organizes comparison scenarios. Pipeline amounts provide exposure context. A high score prompts inspection of payment, charge and volume inputs; its weights have not been validated against purchase decisions or sales outcomes.

## A walkthrough

In the current [demo](https://specialtypulse.kristenmartino.ai), Orthopedic Surgery ranks highest at 70.1 in the constructed scenario. Its synthetic pipeline contains 47 active deals worth $6.27M—15.6% of active pipeline. Selecting a specialty opens its procedures; summary links connect the observations to those views.

The ranking is a scenario result, not a finding about historical Medicare reimbursements. An analyst can inspect how the inputs and weighting produced it before deciding what additional evidence to gather.

## Follow a displayed value to its source

The [raw 2023 fixture](https://github.com/kristenmartino/specialtypulse_pipeline/blob/main/data/sample_2023_puf_10k.csv) contains 57 Dermatology / HCPCS 99213 rows with 3,777 services. Summing payment multiplied by services gives $396,247.22. Dividing by 3,777 and rounding to cents produces **$104.91**.

The shared Spark functions write that result to the mart used by the app. Open [Procedure Detail](https://specialtypulse.kristenmartino.ai/?view=procedure), expand “Verify one calculation,” then find HCPCS 99213 in the normal procedure list: both show the same payment and service count. The [input/output artifact](https://github.com/kristenmartino/specialtypulse_pipeline/blob/main/data/payment_verification.json) lists the source row numbers.

Independent Decimal tests reconcile service totals, provider counts and three weighted payment columns for **all 870 mart rows**. CI also re-executes Spark and compares every business column with the committed output. The app build rejects source, transformation-code or mart hashes that differ from the recorded run.

The [reproduction guide](https://github.com/kristenmartino/specialtypulse_pipeline/blob/main/docs/verification/payment-check.md) contains the commands and complete evidence path.

## Engineering and verification

The first complete run exposed duplicate synthetic provider identifiers. I corrected the fixture IDs deterministically and kept duplicate-key rejection in the shared staging checks. The pipeline validates the grain before aggregation instead of silently dropping conflicting rows.

Policy tests now [import the deployed builders and setup function directly](https://github.com/kristenmartino/specialtypulse_pipeline/blob/main/tests/test_pdp_policy_builders.py). They exercise the real 13-user configuration, policy grouping and schema guard with a fake API client. Importing the module requires no credentials and sends no requests.

The verified execution is local Spark with a Parquet staging round-trip. Databricks catalog/Delta operations, Airflow orchestration and Domo services remain separate integration work; no successful service run is claimed. Public access rules and usage remain illustrative. The [implementation record](https://github.com/kristenmartino/specialtypulse_pipeline/blob/main/docs/PRD.md) separates those states.

## One deployment tradeoff

The Databricks mart adapter checks a temporary table before replacing the previous version. Its drop-and-rename step leaves an availability gap. An operational deployment would need an atomic replacement or a view switch with a tested recovery path.
