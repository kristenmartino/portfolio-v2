## A specialty review for sales or operations

SpecialtyPulse is a prototype for a **specialty analyst preparing a review of payment patterns for a sales or operations lead**. The decision is which patterns need further investigation and what evidence the next reviewer should check before a commercial recommendation.

I designed SpecialtyPulse around that review task: compare specialty measures, inspect procedure detail, and prepare a handoff that states the supporting observations, open questions and who should review them next. It is an independent prototype using synthetic records and comparison scenarios.

## Problem hypothesis to validate

**Assumed current workflow:** an analyst compares payment tables, checks pipeline context in a separate report and copies findings into a brief for a sales or operations lead. Moving between those sources may leave evidence or follow-up requests out of the handoff.

**Product hypothesis:** building the brief during the investigation could reduce preparation time and clarification work while preserving the evidence needed to interpret it. The proposed improvement is a more complete brief that a recipient can act on.

These are assumptions, not research findings. Before a pilot, I would ask intended analysts and recipients to walk through a recent review: how often it happens, which tools they use, how long it takes, what evidence gets lost and which decision follows. If reviews are infrequent or existing reports already meet the need, I would reconsider the product scope.

## What the reviewer hands off

The [demo](https://specialtypulse.kristenmartino.ai/?view=market) includes “Prepare a specialty review · sample handoff.” A shared specialty selection connects the handoff and Procedure Detail. Analysts can compare the same procedure across two consecutive years, add that comparison to the brief, then write an observation and its limits, an open question, a next reviewer and a requested check.

The Markdown download includes both years' payments and service counts, calculated changes, the analyst's draft notes and a link that reopens the selected comparison. Verification links point to the reporting mart, synthetic raw inputs and local Spark receipt at the build's repository revision; hashes identify the recorded data snapshot. Each specialty keeps a separate draft as the analyst switches tabs. Drafts last until the page reloads; downloading keeps a copy and does not send or assign it. Missing fields remain explicitly marked. A four-element completeness check measures presence, not accuracy or approval.

## Worked example: a completed illustrative brief

Open [Dermatology / HCPCS 99213 / CY2023](https://specialtypulse.kristenmartino.ai/?view=procedure&specialty=Dermatology&hcpcs=99213&year=2023), inspect the two-year comparison and choose **Add comparison to brief**. The following is an illustrative completed handoff, not a record of stakeholder use or assignment.

- **Focus and evidence:** Office visit, established patient — Dermatology / HCPCS 99213. The generated 2022 scenario has **$108.05 per service across 3,626 services**; the synthetic 2023 base has **$104.91 across 3,777 services**. Payment changes by **−$3.14 (−2.9%)** and service count by **+151 (+4.2%)**. Percentages use the prior-year value as the denominator; payment differences use the displayed mart amounts rounded to cents.
- **Analyst observation and limits:** In this constructed comparison, payment per service falls while service count rises. The generated prior-year scenario cannot establish a historical payment trend, its cause or an account's need.
- **Open question:** Which source rows and service mix explain this constructed change?
- **Next reviewer:** Specialty analyst.
- **Requested next check:** Match Dermatology / HCPCS 99213 in the pinned 2022 and 2023 inputs and reporting rows. Reproduce the service-weighted payments and service counts, then record unresolved data or interpretation issues for the sales or operations lead.
- **Commercial context:** Dermatology's scenario score is 66.6. Its 53 synthetic active deals total $4,579,800, or 11.4% of active pipeline. Those amounts are separate from the procedure comparison and score.
- **Recipient verification:** The downloaded brief carries the exact comparison link, versioned input/mart files, data hashes and run receipt. Its 2023 calculation trace identifies the 57 raw rows behind the $104.91 value below; that trace does not verify the generated 2022 inputs.
- **Status:** illustrative draft; source and account validation remain pending. Naming a reviewer does not send or assign the brief. No shortlist or stakeholder approval has been recorded.

The recipient's next action is to verify the evidence and report unresolved issues before a commercial recommendation. A specialty-level score does not establish an account's need or justify outreach by itself.

## How usefulness would be evaluated

This workflow and its evaluation criteria are proposed. No stakeholder validation, user evaluation or business outcomes have been recorded. The criteria are also visible in the demo.

I would recruit **five analyst–reviewer pairs for an exploratory comparison**. Each analyst would prepare two briefs from matched data: one with their usual report or spreadsheet and one with SpecialtyPulse. I would alternate the order and scenario assigned to each method, confirming the existing workflow and comparable task difficulty first.

Preparation time would run from opening the data to submitting a brief. Recipients would review briefs without the method identified; I would record missing evidence, interpretation errors and clarification requests before they can state the next action.

The proposed thresholds for proceeding to a larger pilot are:

1. **Completeness:** at least four of five SpecialtyPulse briefs include procedure evidence, an observation with limits, an open question, and a next reviewer with a requested check. Completeness must be at least as high as the comparison method.
2. **Interpretation:** no critical errors in SpecialtyPulse briefs or recipient explanations. Treating synthetic data as observed history, a score as account need, or a blended average as a procedure-specific expected rate would count as a critical error.
3. **Preparation time:** a median paired time reduction of at least 20% against the comparison method, without reducing completeness.
4. **Clarification:** at least four of five recipients can explain the next action without clarification, with no more requests overall than for the comparison briefs.

These thresholds are proposed, not achieved results. If accuracy or completeness fails, I would revise the workflow before testing speed again. Sessions and page views describe activity; they do not demonstrate usefulness. The [workflow and evaluation plan](https://github.com/kristenmartino/specialtypulse_pipeline/blob/main/docs/BUSINESS_REVIEW.md) documents the proposed process.

## Decisions behind the workflow

**Make the payment denominator explicit.** Average payment per billed service gives larger service volumes more weight. In the reconciled example, a simple average across 57 source rows is $103.80; weighting by their 3,777 services gives $104.91. Different denominators can lead reviewers to different conclusions.

**Keep procedure comparisons specific.** The comparison matches specialty, HCPCS code and consecutive years. Missing inputs or a zero prior value leave percentage changes unavailable. I also rank the most-used procedures by service count. A specialty's blended average combines different procedures, so a payment below that blend is not evidence that a particular procedure is underpaid. The ratio-based outlier definition is available beside the table.

**Keep scores and sales dollars separate.** The index organizes comparison scenarios; pipeline amounts provide exposure context. The score prompts questions about payment, charge and volume inputs. Its weights have not been validated against purchase decisions or sales outcomes.

## Scope and next validation

All records are synthetic. The 2021–2022 inputs are generated comparison scenarios, not historical CMS observations. The verified run uses local Spark and a Parquet staging round-trip. Databricks catalog/Delta operations, Airflow orchestration and Domo services remain separately unverified; public access rules and usage are illustrative.

The Databricks mart adapter's drop-and-rename replacement also leaves an availability gap. Operational use would require an atomic replacement or view switch, tested recovery, real-source reconciliation and intended-user access checks.

Next, I would validate the assumed workflow and run the comparison study, then verify external integrations before operational recommendations. [GTM Healthcare Intelligence](/work/gtm-healthcare) covers the related reporting-workflow prototype.
