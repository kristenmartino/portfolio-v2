## A specialty review for sales or operations

SpecialtyPulse is an independent prototype for a **specialty analyst supporting a healthcare-software sales team**. The assumed employer sells electronic health record (EHR), practice management (PM) and revenue cycle management (RCM) subscriptions to specialty practices. A sales or operations lead uses the brief to decide which questions need analyst verification or account discovery before a software recommendation.

Medicare payment comparisons describe practice-side signals; pipeline dollars represent hypothetical software contracts for the vendor. Account conversations must establish customer needs before recommending software or prioritizing outreach. This business setting is a design assumption, not a finding from customer research.

## Problem hypothesis to validate

**Assumed current workflow:** an analyst compares payment tables, checks pipeline context in a separate report and copies findings into a brief for a sales or operations lead. Moving between those sources may leave evidence or follow-up requests out of the handoff.

**Product hypothesis:** building the brief during the investigation could reduce preparation time and clarification work while preserving the evidence needed to interpret it. The proposed improvement is a more complete brief that a recipient can act on.

These are assumptions, not research findings. Before a pilot, I would ask intended analysts and recipients to walk through a recent review: how often it happens, which tools they use, how long it takes, what evidence gets lost and which decision follows. If reviews are infrequent or existing reports already meet the need, I would reconsider the product scope.

## What the analyst hands off

In the [demo](https://specialtypulse.kristenmartino.ai/?view=market), choose **Start review** under **Specialty review**. A shared specialty selection connects the handoff, Procedure Detail and Pipeline Intelligence. The pipeline review keeps that specialty’s totals, owner amounts and overdue opportunity list together. A separately labeled chart compares all specialties; “Return to brief” reopens the current specialty’s draft. Analysts can compare the same procedure across two consecutive years, add that comparison to the brief, then write an observation and its limits, an open question, a next reviewer and a requested check. **Method and sources** opens the comparison formulas and evidence links; **About this demo** holds the general assumptions.

The Markdown download includes both years' payments and service counts, calculated changes, the analyst's draft notes and a link that reopens the selected comparison. It also includes owner totals and the complete specialty-scoped overdue opportunity list. A separate overdue CSV preserves opportunity IDs, owners, stages, assumed ACV, dates, overdue ages, the scenario date and the source revision. Both exports use the full cohort, regardless of table pagination. Verification links point to the reporting mart, synthetic raw inputs, sales CSV, metric definitions and the selected run receipt at the build's repository revision; hashes identify the recorded data snapshot. Each specialty keeps a separate draft as the analyst switches tabs. Drafts last until the page reloads; downloading keeps a copy and does not send or assign it. Missing fields remain explicitly marked. A four-element completeness check measures presence, not accuracy or approval.

- **Requested next check:** First reconcile the 53 Dermatology open opportunities and their past-due close dates with sales operations. Then match Dermatology / HCPCS 99213 in the pinned 2022 and 2023 inputs and reporting rows. Reproduce the service-weighted payments and service counts, then record unresolved data or interpretation issues for the sales or operations lead.
- **Commercial context:** Dermatology's scenario score is 66.6. Its 53 synthetic open deals total $4,579,800 in assumed annual contract value (USD), or 11.4% of open-stage pipeline. All 53 have overdue close dates at the scenario snapshot and remain in the total pending review. Those amounts are separate from the procedure comparison and score.
- **Recipient verification:** The downloaded brief carries the exact comparison link, versioned input/mart files, data hashes and run receipt. Its 2023 calculation trace identifies the 57 raw rows behind the $104.91 value below; that trace does not verify the generated 2022 inputs.

## Deployment path

The current stack is **Databricks → Airflow-validated export → React dashboard**. I archived the earlier Domo integration after trial access ended. The React interface and its review workflow remain in place.

The serverless Databricks job uses the shared Spark transformations, writes raw, staging and mart Delta tables, and compares every mart business value with the reconciled baseline. Airflow checks the successful run's receipt and exported file, saves an immutable versioned copy, and selects it for the next React build. The build checks the selected export again. Creating a scheduled export and publishing a website release are separate steps.

## Decisions behind the workflow

**Make the payment denominator explicit.** Average payment per billed service gives larger service volumes more weight. In the reconciled example, a simple average across 57 source rows is $103.80; weighting by their 3,777 services gives $104.91. Different denominators can lead reviewers to different conclusions.

**Keep procedure comparisons specific.** The comparison matches specialty, HCPCS code and consecutive years. Missing inputs or a zero prior value leave percentage changes unavailable. I also rank the most-used procedures by service count. A specialty's blended average combines different procedures, so a payment below that blend is not evidence that a particular procedure is underpaid. The ratio-based outlier definition is available beside the table.

**Count providers at the decision grain.** A provider billing several procedures counts once within each specialty and year. Spark computes that distinct count from staged provider IDs before procedure aggregation; the dashboard and pressure index use it once. A regression with overlapping procedures checks this behavior, and independent raw-input reconciliation verifies every published specialty/year count.

**Keep scores and sales dollars separate.** The index organizes comparison scenarios; pipeline amounts provide exposure context. The score prompts questions about payment, charge and volume inputs. Its weights have not been validated against purchase decisions or sales outcomes.

**Define the sales dollar basis and date scope.** For this demo, opportunity amount is assumed annual contract value (ACV) in USD. Open-stage totals are unweighted, exclude Closed Won and Closed Lost, and include overdue open opportunities. Closed-won ACV is a separate stage total, not recognized revenue or a forecast.

The **invented sales scenario snapshot is August 31, 2026**; the source capture date was not recorded. Stages remain as supplied. All 500 fixture opportunities are included without a reporting-period filter: created dates September 2, 2025–February 24, 2026; close dates October 11, 2025–August 14, 2026. These sales dates are separate from reimbursement CY2021–2023.

**Surface the overdue backlog before planning.** At that scenario date, all **472 open opportunities, totaling $40,178,200 in assumed ACV**, have close dates in the past: **100% of open deals, 17–324 days overdue**. The dashboard shows the overdue count and ACV. Its specialty-scoped review list exposes the underlying opportunities and can be sorted by owner, stage, amount, close date or overdue age. Each specialty brief carries its own overdue exposure. The list is a review aid; it does not write changes to a CRM. The first sales follow-up is to reconcile stages and close dates with sales operations. The prototype retains the supplied opportunities in the open-stage total until reviewed; past-due dates alone do not close or remove a deal. Missing or invalid close dates are reported separately. No actual sales review or CRM update has been recorded.

## AI comparison results · human validation pending

On October 26, 2026, **five fictional AI analysts and five separate AI reviewers** completed an artifact-based comparison. Each analyst prepared one brief from constructed CSV reports and one from a captured app draft. Both methods produced **5 of 5 complete briefs**, with **100 of 100 structured fields correct per method**. Reviewers could state the next action for all ten briefs without clarification; no critical interpretation errors were found. **No advantage for SpecialtyPulse was demonstrated.**

The agents had fresh contexts but shared a model family. Their role-based workflows were assumed, and source cues prevented full method blinding. Analysts did not navigate the app. Runtime excluded source gathering, coordinator preparation, browser use and reviewer work, so it cannot estimate human time savings or assess the proposed 20% threshold. **Human validation remains 0 of 5 pairs.**

## Planned human comparison

The five-pair human comparison has not started. Its evaluation criteria remain proposed and are documented below and in the linked evaluation plan. No stakeholder validation or human user-evaluation results have been recorded.

I would recruit **five analyst–reviewer pairs for an exploratory comparison**. Each analyst would prepare two briefs from matched data: one with their usual report or spreadsheet and one with SpecialtyPulse. I would alternate the order and scenario assigned to each method, confirming the existing workflow and comparable task difficulty first.

Preparation time would cover the full workflow for both methods: receiving the business question, locating and exporting source data, checking calculations and delivering the brief. Recipients would review briefs without the method identified; I would record missing evidence, interpretation errors and clarification requests before they can state the next action.

The proposed thresholds for proceeding to a larger pilot are:

1. **Completeness:** at least four of five SpecialtyPulse briefs include procedure evidence, an observation with limits, an open question, and a next reviewer with a requested check. Completeness must be at least as high as the comparison method.
2. **Interpretation:** no critical errors in SpecialtyPulse briefs or recipient explanations. Treating synthetic data as observed history, a score as account need, or a blended average as a procedure-specific expected rate would count as a critical error.
3. **Preparation time:** a median paired time reduction of at least 20% against the comparison method, without reducing completeness.
4. **Clarification:** at least four of five recipients can explain the next action without clarification, with no more requests overall than for the comparison briefs.

These thresholds are proposed, not achieved results. If accuracy or completeness fails, I would revise the workflow before testing speed again. Sessions and page views describe activity; they do not demonstrate usefulness.
