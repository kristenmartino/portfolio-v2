## The business question

Which payment changes need investigation, and what should a sales or operations lead check next? SpecialtyPulse is an independent prototype for an analyst supporting a healthcare-software vendor selling EHR, practice management and revenue cycle subscriptions.

The assumed workflow combines payment reports, CRM exports and a written handoff. The hypothesis is that preparing an internal sales-review packet requires carrying evidence and reviewer questions across those tools. SpecialtyPulse tests that handoff alongside existing reporting. Intended users have not confirmed the workflow or its pain points; if their current reports and CRM already handle it well, a separate tool may add little.

**All data is synthetic.** The 2023 base uses a CMS-style schema; 2021–2022 are generated comparison scenarios. Payment signals describe the practice side, while pipeline dollars represent hypothetical software contracts. Account discovery is still needed before a software recommendation or outreach decision.

## One review, from signal to next check

For [Dermatology / HCPCS 99213](https://specialtypulse.kristenmartino.ai/?view=procedure&specialty=Dermatology&hcpcs=99213&year=2023), the generated 2022 scenario has **$108.05 per service across 3,626 services**. The synthetic 2023 base has **$104.91 across 3,777 services**: payment falls **$3.14 (2.9%)**, while service count rises **151 (4.2%)**.

An illustrative brief records that change, asks which source rows and service mix explain it, and requests a calculation check from the analyst. Its separate sales context shows **53 open opportunities totaling $4,579,800 in assumed USD annual contract value (ACV)**. At the constructed **March 31, 2026** snapshot, **25 are overdue, totaling $2,121,500**, while 28 are not yet overdue. Sales operations must reconcile stages and close dates before planning. These amounts are unweighted exposure, not recognized revenue or a forecast.

This is an illustrative assignment, with source and account validation pending. The specialty score of 66.6 organizes scenarios; its weights have not been validated against account need, purchase decisions or sales outcomes.

Two [fictional Dermatology account examples](https://specialtypulse.kristenmartino.ai/?view=market&specialty=Dermatology&section=accounts) show why the same payment signal can lead to different next checks. The link opens the brief with **Compare two fictional practices** expanded. Each practice puts its decision, next reviewer and requested check before the supporting detail. Practice A's invented notes and work log describe duplicate billing-status entry and support a workflow review, with software fit pending. Each includes a stakeholder, current systems, baseline, source excerpts and open questions. Either example can accompany the downloaded brief without replacing the analyst's notes.

## What was verified

On October 26, Databricks Spark 4.2.0 processed **29,982 raw records into 870 reporting rows**, matching every business value in the reconciled baseline. All four tasks in the clean Airflow 3.1.8 run passed on their first attempt. The public React dashboard uses that run's versioned export. [Execution records](https://github.com/kristenmartino/specialtypulse_pipeline/blob/dcee05b9b85c50c0536ddb61cc5dffcefd2a0853/data/deployment_verification.json) document the run.

The displayed $104.91 reconciles to **57 raw rows**, a weighted payment sum of **$396,247.22** and **3,777 services**. A simple row average would be $103.80. Spark counts distinct providers at specialty/year grain before procedure aggregation, so billing several procedures does not multiply the provider count. Independent Decimal checks cover all 870 groups; CI reruns the shared Spark transformations.

Airflow runs on a local Mac that must be awake and signed in with valid OAuth access. New exports require a separate website release. Public usage and access rules are illustrative; the demo serves the same synthetic data to everyone.

## AI comparison: quality tied

On October 26, **five fictional AI analysts and five separate AI reviewers** completed ten briefs using constructed CSV reports and captured app drafts. Both methods produced **5 of 5 complete briefs**, with **100 of 100 structured fields correct per method**. Reviewers could state the next action for all ten briefs without clarification; no critical interpretation errors were found. **No advantage for SpecialtyPulse was demonstrated.**

This was an artifact simulation: agents did not navigate the browser, and timing excluded source gathering, coordinator preparation and reviewer work. Fresh contexts shared a model family, role-based workflows were assumed, and source cues prevented full method blinding. The test remains pinned to **its original August 31, 2026 scenario and source revision d628a13**, before the UI cleanup, recipient packet and March sales scenario. It was not rerun for these changes and cannot estimate human time savings.

**Human validation remains 0 of 5 planned pairs.** Usefulness, adoption and business outcomes are unmeasured. The [comparison report and audit records](/work/specialtypulse-ai-comparison-2026-10-26/results.html) include fictional role contexts, briefs, scoring and limitations. An [earlier single-assistant rehearsal](/work/specialtypulse-rehearsal-2026-10-25/results.html) found and verified a missing sales-source link.

<summary className="cursor-pointer font-semibold text-[var(--color-bone)]">Metric definitions and sales scope</summary>

**Payment grain.** Raw payments are weighted by service count within specialty, HCPCS and year. Year comparisons use the previous year's denominator and displayed mart values rounded to cents. Missing inputs, duplicate matches or zero prior denominators leave changes unavailable. Specialty averages combine procedure-level measures; a payment below that blend is not evidence that a specific procedure is underpaid. Ratio outliers use the specialty/year distribution.

**Provider grain.** A provider counts once per specialty/year across procedures. An overlapping-procedure regression and independent raw-input reconciliation check every published specialty/year count.

**Sales basis.** Open-stage totals exclude Closed Won and Closed Lost and retain overdue opportunities. Closed-won ACV is a separate stage total. All 500 fixture opportunities are in scope, without a reporting-period filter; created dates span September 2, 2025–February 24, 2026 and close dates October 11, 2025–August 14, 2026. Dates and stages are constructed to be consistent with March 31, not captured or reconstructed CRM history. Closed-stage dates represent completion within the synthetic scenario; open-stage dates are expected. No opportunity is created after the snapshot, closed after the snapshot, or assigned a stage age longer than its existence. The build checks those relationships and the recorded fixture hash.

**Overdue scope.** At March 31, the 472 open deals total $40,178,200 in assumed ACV. Of these, **278 are overdue by 1–171 days ($23,546,900)** and **194 are not yet overdue ($16,631,300)**. Only valid close dates strictly before that snapshot qualify; missing or invalid dates stay separate. Past-due dates alone do not close or remove deals. Owner ages use eligible overdue dates; an em dash means none. Sales records cover nine specialties, versus 29 in reimbursement reporting. No supplied specialty rows means unavailable coverage, not zero sales.

The revision preserved IDs, amounts, stages and open-deal dates. It moved 12 future closed-stage dates before March 31 using their supplied stage ages, then aligned the other 16 closed-stage ages with their existing close dates. The stack is **Databricks → Airflow-validated export → React**. The earlier Domo integration was archived after trial access ended. The serverless job writes raw, staging and mart Delta tables using the same transformations as the local Spark/Parquet baseline. Airflow checks the receipt and export, saves an immutable versioned copy, and selects it for the next React build. The build rejects mismatched source, transformation or output hashes. Release checks also reject changed artifacts for a recorded run.

The first complete run exposed duplicate synthetic provider identifiers. These were corrected deterministically, with duplicate-key rejection retained before aggregation. Both the initial scheduled cloud refresh and a separate clean manual run completed; the public export comes from the clean run.

The local Mac's annual UTC schedule is enabled; its next run is due **December 31, 2026 at 7 p.m. Eastern**. Host availability and valid OAuth remain dependencies. The three Delta tables are replaced in individual commits, not one shared transaction. Operational use still requires real-source reconciliation, intended-user access enforcement, recovery tests and an alerting plan. The review list does not write to a CRM, and no actual sales review or CRM update has been recorded.

<summary className="cursor-pointer font-semibold text-[var(--color-bone)]">Proposed human comparison criteria</summary>

Confirm actual workflows and comparable task difficulty before testing. Recipients review briefs without the method identified; record their next-action explanations, missing evidence, interpretation errors and clarification requests. Proposed thresholds for a larger pilot are:

1. At least four of five app briefs include procedure evidence, an observation with limits, an open question and a next reviewer with a concrete check; completeness is at least as high as the comparator.
2. No critical interpretation errors, including synthetic data treated as history, a score treated as account need or a blended average treated as a procedure-specific expected rate.
3. At least 20% median paired preparation-time reduction over the full workflow, without reducing completeness.
4. At least four of five recipients explain the next action without clarification, with no more requests overall than for comparator briefs.

These are proposed criteria, not achieved human results. Accuracy or completeness failures require revision before retesting speed. Page views and sessions are activity measures, not usefulness evidence.
