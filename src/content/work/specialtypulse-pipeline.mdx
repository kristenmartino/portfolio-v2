## A specialty review for sales or operations

The proposed user is a **specialty analyst preparing a payment-pattern review for a sales or operations lead**. The decision is which patterns need further investigation and what evidence the next reviewer should check before a commercial recommendation.

I designed SpecialtyPulse around that review task: compare specialty measures, inspect procedure detail, and prepare a handoff with supporting observations, unanswered questions and a receiving role. It is an independent prototype using synthetic records and comparison scenarios. Stakeholder use and business impact have not been measured.

## What the reviewer hands off

The [demo](https://specialtypulse.kristenmartino.ai/?view=market) includes “Prepare a specialty review · sample handoff.” Selecting a specialty updates its observations. The analyst can inspect the related procedures, compare pipeline exposure and download a sample brief.

In the current constructed scenario, the example starts with Orthopedic Surgery:

- **Focus:** investigate its payment comparison. The scenario score is 70.1 and average procedure-level payment change is −3.9%.
- **Exposure context:** 47 synthetic active deals worth $6.27M, or 15.6% of active pipeline. Those amounts are separate from the score.
- **Questions:** do source records, service mix and calculation explain the comparison? Are opportunity stages current, and what account needs support further review?
- **Proposed handoff:** the specialty analyst checks the source and interpretation; sales operations checks deal/account context; the receiving sales or operations lead confirms the next reviewer and evidence request.
- **Status:** sample only, with source and account validation pending. This is a question-led review brief, not an approved account shortlist or a record of stakeholder sign-off.

The intended output is a clearer request for follow-up. A specialty-level score does not establish an account's need or justify outreach by itself.

## How usefulness would be evaluated

These are **proposed acceptance criteria**, also visible in the demo. No user-test results, adoption improvements or time savings are claimed.

1. **Explain the focus.** An intended reviewer identifies the selected specialty, cites a payment comparison and pipeline exposure, and recognizes that the inputs are synthetic.
2. **Interpret the measures.** The reviewer explains the payment denominator and distinguishes a scenario score from a sales recommendation.
3. **Make a usable handoff.** The brief names an unresolved question, a receiving role and a next check. The receiving reviewer can identify what follow-up is requested.

In a future evaluation, I would ask an intended reviewer to prepare a brief, observe where coaching is needed, and ask the recipient to explain the requested action. I would record interpretation errors, missing evidence, unanswered questions and recipient feedback before making claims about business usefulness. The [workflow and evaluation plan](https://github.com/kristenmartino/specialtypulse_pipeline/blob/main/docs/BUSINESS_REVIEW.md) documents that proposed process.

## Decisions behind the workflow

**Make the payment denominator explicit.** Average payment per billed service gives larger service volumes more weight. In the reconciled example, a simple average across 57 source rows is $103.80; weighting by their 3,777 services gives $104.91. Different denominators can lead reviewers to different conclusions.

**Keep procedure comparisons specific.** I rank the most-used procedures by service count and display payment alongside them. A specialty's blended average combines different procedures, so a payment below that blend is not evidence that a particular procedure is underpaid. The ratio-based outlier definition is available beside the table.

**Keep scores and sales dollars separate.** The index organizes comparison scenarios; pipeline amounts provide exposure context. The score prompts questions about payment, charge and volume inputs. Its weights have not been validated against purchase decisions or sales outcomes.

## Evidence supporting the review

The public procedure list displays **$104.91 across 3,777 services** for Dermatology / HCPCS 99213. The 57 matching raw rows have a weighted payment sum of $396,247.22. The displayed value reconciles to those records, the exported reporting table and the browser data.

A recorded local Spark run processes **29,982 raw records into 870 rows used by the dashboard**. The Databricks notebook adapters import the same transformation functions. Independent Decimal tests reconcile all 870 groups, and CI re-executes Spark to compare every business column. The build rejects source, transformation-code or output hashes that differ from the recorded run.

## Scope and next validation

All records are synthetic. The 2021–2022 inputs are generated comparison scenarios, not historical CMS observations. The verified run uses local Spark and a Parquet staging round-trip. Databricks catalog/Delta operations, Airflow orchestration and Domo services remain separately unverified; public access rules and usage are illustrative.

The Databricks mart adapter's drop-and-rename replacement also leaves an availability gap. Operational use would require an atomic replacement or view switch, tested recovery, real-source reconciliation and intended-user access checks.

My next business-validation step would be the proposed reviewer-and-recipient exercise above. External integrations and outcome validation would follow before operational recommendations. [GTM Healthcare Intelligence](/work/gtm-healthcare) covers the related reporting-workflow prototype.
