## The problem

A valuation can calculate correctly while relying on the wrong input. SEC filers use different XBRL concepts for similar figures, and some required values are available only in filing text. The product needs to expose those extraction choices so a reviewer can examine the inputs before relying on the estimate.

## 1. Keep partial extraction results

The first extraction stage queries SEC XBRL company facts using a concept map. Berkshire also needs dimensioned facts from the filing itself: a deterministic inline-XBRL reader selects annual equivalent shares for the requested class, insurance premiums, and cash across its two reported business groups. It returns the values it finds and leaves missing fields for Claude to extract from the filing's financial-statements section. Required fields still missing after the fallback and supported calculations produce an explicit error.

This matters because failing on the first missing XBRL field would prevent the fallback from running. In the original ten-company sample, three needed AI extraction or a calculated fallback. Those figures describe the ten-company sample only.

## 2. Show how each value was obtained

Each line item identifies its source: SEC XBRL, AI extraction, calculation, or user correction. Source details show an XBRL concept, an extracted excerpt, or a calculation explanation where available. Reviewers still need to check each value against the filing.

Automated checks flag extraction scores below 0.80 and balance-sheet discrepancies above 0.5%. The scores are heuristics: XBRL scores reflect tag priority, while AI scores are model-reported. They are not calibrated probabilities of correctness, and an absence of flags does not mean a person verified the data.

The balance-sheet check keeps parent equity and noncontrolling interests separate, adding them when required without counting the component twice for consolidated equity. This resolves Prologis's false reconciliation warning while preserving the reported figures and valuation inputs. A missing component is accepted only from matching annual-filing facts; unavailable or ambiguous coverage still needs review.

AAPL, CRM and CSCO exposed debt totals that included current maturities already carried in short-term debt. Filing-backed mappings now select noncurrent debt for these companies. CRM also needed total cash-flow D&A and an explicit combined payables/accruals label. Repairs preserve reviewed corrections and feed corrected debt into the net-debt bridge and default WACC calculation. These targeted checks are separate from the historical extraction baseline.

CSCO's generic D&A tag also covered property and equipment only. Its model now uses the filed annual depreciation, amortization and other cash-flow adjustment across the saved history. This is a combined proxy, not pure D&A; statement rows, the assumption ratio and projection column name that scope. Matching tagged annual contexts are required, and the repair preserves corrected debt and the reviewed SG&A note. The historical adjustment/revenue ratio changes the cash-flow estimate without changing the debt bridge or other assumptions.

CRM's revenue display also needs complete coverage: its five subscription offerings exclude professional services. The workspace reads all six categories from tagged filing contexts, checks them against the subscription subtotal and consolidated total, and avoids adding the subtotal twice. It labels these as revenue categories because Salesforce reports one operating segment. This display does not change the consolidated valuation.

IBM exposed a different gap: its statements are incorporated in an annual-report exhibit and its standard operating-income tag is absent. A targeted calculation uses filed GAAP pre-tax income from continuing operations plus separately reported interest as an EBIT proxy. The workspace labels the proxy in its figure, assumptions, statements, sensitivity and projection, explains the retained non-operating items and financing costs, and keeps it flagged for review. It is neither reported operating income nor IBM's operating (non-GAAP) earnings.

The same filing also supplies current borrowings and both components of cash-flow D&A. Missing or conflicting required inputs still prevent valuation. The model note discloses that capex covers PP&E only and excludes separately reported software investment.

INTU exposed similar gaps on a new filer: depreciation alone omitted acquired-intangible amortization, and an inclusive debt tag counted current maturities twice. Its mapping now sums the full filed cash-flow D&A components ($187M + $659M for FY2026) and separates noncurrent debt ($6.420B) from current maturities ($1.249B). Matching annual facts cover the historical ratio; saved repair preserves reviewer corrections and rejects incomplete or conflicting coverage. Its model note retains the PP&E-only capex scope, excluding capitalized internal-use software. These targeted checks do not expand the historical extraction baseline or manually approve other inputs.

MSFT showed why the scope of a tagged value still needs checking: its depreciation tag omitted intangible amortization. The mapping now adds the separate expense disclosures ($34.3B + $4.7B for FY2026), with the same scope across the historical ratio. The company note and source details distinguish this pure D&A sum from the cash-flow row that also includes other adjustments. Its inclusive debt total also appeared in the long-term slot while current debt was blank; splitting $9.227B current and $31.067B noncurrent together preserves the $40.294B aggregate and avoids introducing a double-count. The D&A correction changes the estimate; the debt split leaves net debt and WACC unchanged. Matching annual facts, atomic history repair and review-preservation rules apply without expanding the historical extraction baseline or approving other fields.

A reviewer can replace a flagged value and optionally supply a supporting excerpt. Validation then runs again. The stored company record retains the current correction and its source label; it does not provide reviewer identity or a chronological edit history.

The REIT model approximates FFO as net income plus D&A. Its supplementary AFFO estimate subtracts positive reported capital expenditures, or uses 80% of approximate FFO when those expenditures are unavailable. Both calculations are labeled as estimates in the workspace.

Berkshire's Class A and B workspaces use the simplified justified-price-to-book formula with consolidated GAAP earnings and Berkshire shareholders' equity. This is not a sum-of-the-parts valuation. Investment gains can distort ROE, and per-share amounts use reported annual average equivalent shares rather than year-end shares. The workspace names the share basis and labels investment and policy-liability components according to their source concepts. Its cash calculation retains Treasury bills already classified as cash equivalents and excludes separately reported short-term investments. This targeted filing regression is separate from the historical extraction baseline below.

The workspace pairs the estimate with its assumptions. Slider ranges include the starting values, including TXN's capex/revenue assumption above the usual range. A restore action returns all assumptions to the full-precision values loaded when the workspace opened, so reviewers can return to the starting scenario after making changes.

Cash-flow controls distinguish model assumptions from filed ratios. Standard DCF averages eligible tax/pre-tax ratios after limiting each to 0–50%, then limits the average to 15–30%; missing tax data uses 21%. IBM's 15% starting tax assumption therefore differs from its filed FY2025 effective rate of −2.3%. Energy omits the additional 15–30% normalization. Working capital starts at a fixed 5% of the change in revenue, rather than a ratio inferred from the company's balances. Capex and cash-flow add-back ratios average available annual inputs, with disclosed fallbacks. Read-only tax context for banks, insurers and REITs is labeled separately and does not drive those models. These disclosures explain the existing calculations without changing model values or saved reviews.

Monte Carlo output shows the distribution produced by sampled inputs; its middle 80% is a simulation range, not a calibrated forecast of the stock price. Standard and energy models also show a sensitivity grid.

The peer table provides Yahoo Finance market context separately from the selected model. Its median uses only positive values from returned peers, excluding missing, zero and negative multiples, along with the target and DCF-implied rows. Each metric shows how many peers contributed. Individual negative values remain visible. Market data is requested when the workspace loads, but the panel does not expose provider as-of timestamps, so a new request does not establish that every input is current.

The Claude miss was an omitted pre-tax-income value. The small AI sample is a material limitation; the combined result should not be interpreted as general AI extraction accuracy. This is a historical baseline, not a new evaluation run.

The code includes deterministic valuation tests and correction tests across statement variants. One network-gated integration test exercises a real Apple filing. These checks cover specific behaviors, rather than every supported filer or financial model assumption.

The remaining limits affect how the tool should be used. Unclassified companies fall back to the standard DCF even when that model may not fit. Older periods can have incomplete field coverage. The energy model uses a fixed ten-year horizon rather than a company-specific reserve forecast. Filing freshness, broader extraction evaluation, and correction history need further work before this could support a production research workflow.
