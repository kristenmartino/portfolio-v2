## What I built

GTM Healthcare Intelligence explores two review tasks for specialty healthcare sales and operations: finding where a sales funnel loses momentum and resolving conflicting metric definitions. Five supporting demos cover market comparisons, practice measures and questions about the sample data.

## Two review workflows

**ConvertPath** compares conversion and sales-cycle measures by specialty, source and quarter. Its completed lead cohorts are simulated; the deal-health view uses a separate synthetic scoring sample. Changes shown in improvement scenarios are assumptions, with no measured revenue impact.

**NorthStar** brings competing metric definitions, named owners and simulated reporting-adoption snapshots into one review. Statistical alerts include templated investigation steps. Those steps suggest checks; they do not establish the cause of a change.

## Supporting demos and question answering

SpectrumIQ compares metros using synthetic provider counts and embedded demographic reference values. PracticeFlow uses synthetic peer cohorts, and the GTM version of SpecialtyPulse uses hand-set procedure series.

**AskPractice** calculates answers from fictional practice aggregates, including provider and period filters, without a model call. **AskGTM** calculates supported questions from the same scenario as the sample quarterly review, then uses Claude over supplied scenario text for other questions. It does not execute SQL or query a warehouse. The [AskPractice route](https://github.com/kristenmartino/gtm-healthcare-intel/blob/9f175ded8846f79b72aa99d54df022974dd127b1/app/api/ask/route.js) and [AskGTM route](https://github.com/kristenmartino/gtm-healthcare-intel/blob/9f175ded8846f79b72aa99d54df022974dd127b1/app/api/askgtm/route.js) show that split.

## Data provenance and scope

The demos contain no patient or company records. SpectrumIQ's [checked-in run metadata](https://github.com/kristenmartino/gtm-healthcare-intel/blob/9f175ded8846f79b72aa99d54df022974dd127b1/data/output/pipeline_metadata.json) records synthetic providers and embedded reference values. CMS and Census references describe intended source families; they do not establish a live connection or a completed public-data import. The Python pipeline also supports local NPPES and Census input files.

The [SpecialtyPulse Pipeline case study](/work/specialtypulse-pipeline) covers a separate Spark pipeline and dashboard. Its reporting tables do not feed the hand-set series in this GTM demo.

These exercises demonstrate review workflows. Team adoption, forecast accuracy and sales lift remain unmeasured; operational use would require source reconciliation and evaluation with the intended users.
