## Overview

Cabana is a demo portal for a fictional pool-service company. Customers can report problems, track repairs and pay test deposits.

AI classifies requests and drafts acknowledgments for staff. Eligible repair classifications can open a fixed $75 test deposit step before staff approval. Staff approve work and assign visit times.

Next.js provides the member portal. Supabase Postgres stores member and booking data, with row-level security restricting access to each member’s records.

Edge functions validate incoming Stripe, Telegram and Airtable events. n8n processes pending outbox events to update Airtable and calls a private Supabase function to send Telegram notifications. That function records Telegram’s message ID after a successful send. Claude Haiku classifies requests and drafts acknowledgments for staff. Member status messages use recorded booking and payment states.

## Problem framing

Three risks shaped the implementation:

1. **Failed integrations:** A booking change may succeed while an update to Airtable or a staff notification fails.
2. **Payment confirmation:** Customers may close their browser after paying, and Stripe can resend the same event. Payment recording needs to handle both cases.
3. **AI mistakes:** A misclassified request can reach the test deposit step. Staff approval and visit times remain separate decisions, and AI drafts stay off member status pages.

## AI request triage

Claude Haiku classifies free-text requests and drafts acknowledgments for staff. The application validates the response structure, then applies a [routing policy](https://github.com/kristenmartino/cabana/blob/e9c447e029d50faaf14206a8a48cb5650f2d5e7f/lib/triage/schema.ts). Its confidence score comes from the model; it does not measure observed classification accuracy.

A repair classification with a score of at least 0.8 can open the fixed $75 Stripe test deposit step unless it is flagged as out of the service area. This happens before staff approval. The [request action](https://github.com/kristenmartino/cabana/blob/e9c447e029d50faaf14206a8a48cb5650f2d5e7f/app/request/actions.ts) routes other classifications, low scores, timeouts and invalid responses to review. Staff still approve the work and assign its visit time.

The [20-case golden suite](https://github.com/kristenmartino/cabana/blob/e9c447e029d50faaf14206a8a48cb5650f2d5e7f/tests/golden/golden.test.ts) checks expected routes and forbidden phrases in draft acknowledgments, including prompt-injection cases. It is a regression sample, rather than a general accuracy or containment result.

## Member status

The dashboard shows the regular service day from the member’s plan. The request page shows the stored booking status, payment receipt and any assigned visit window separately. AI triage and Dana’s review are separate steps.

A request that reaches scheduling without an assigned visit window shows “Awaiting visit time,” or “Approved · time pending” after approval. Payment, staff approval and a confirmed visit time are separate states.

When a visit window is recorded before approval, the app labels it “Awaiting approval” and shows the proposed time separately.

## Deposit confirmation

Cabana uses Stripe Checkout in test mode for $75 repair deposits. Payment status is updated from Stripe webhook events after their signatures are verified.

The application records event IDs so it can recognize repeated deliveries and avoid applying the same payment update twice. The confirmation page checks the database and displays “confirming…” until payment is recorded.

## Telegram staff approval

Staff can use **Approve** or **Needs info** on a Telegram notification. Approve records staff approval, updates the existing Airtable booking record and clears the message’s buttons. When a visit time is still unassigned, the member dashboard and request page show “Approved · time pending.” The payment receipt stays separate.

Needs info displays “Noted — reach out to the member for details.” It leaves the booking status unchanged; staff still need to contact the member.

## The transactional outbox

Booking status changes and their pending integration events are saved in the same database transaction. This record of pending work is the transactional outbox.

n8n processes events to update Airtable and send notifications. Failed deliveries are retried, and deduplication keys identify repeated deliveries. Events that reach the retry limit move to a dead-letter queue for operator review.

A webhook trigger starts processing, while a 60-second sweep checks for pending events. Nightly reconciliation checks for unresolved work.

## Failure-injection testing

A recorded test used 50 bookings while the n8n consumer was stopped, Airtable authentication was interrupted and Stripe events were replayed.

All 50 booking records appeared in Airtable with no duplicates. The log also recorded 43 dead-lettered outbox events and no duplicate booking-created events or status transitions.

The test exposed a retry bug that caused repeated alerts for dead-lettered events. The sweep was updated to skip those events until an operator explicitly requeues them.

These results come from one recorded run at demo volume.

## Demo scope

The public demo uses fictional data, a shared demo member account and Stripe test mode. Requests and edits are saved to that shared account, so visitors should use sample details. Row-level security restricts the demo session to that member’s records.

The deposit screen includes a test card number and instructions for completing Stripe’s sandbox checkout. No real money is collected.

**Live check — October 24, 2026:** Request submission, a $75 Stripe test payment, one matching Airtable record and all three Telegram notifications were verified. Telegram sends now run through Supabase after connection timeouts from Railway. The two previously failed notifications were retried successfully, with Telegram message receipts recorded and their failure history retained.

Both buttons were then tested in a signed-in Telegram staff chat. Needs info left the booking unchanged. Approve recorded one transition from `scheduled` to `confirmed`, attributed to `owner:telegram`. The same Airtable row updated without a duplicate, the app showed “Approved · time pending,” and the $75 test deposit remained paid. The approval notification reached Telegram, and the live health check showed no pending or dead-lettered events.

Member email notifications are not implemented. The Telegram bot rejects and logs unauthorized chats; rate limiting and alerts for those attempts remain follow-up work.
