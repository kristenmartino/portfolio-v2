## Overview

Cabana is a demo portal for a fictional pool-service company. Customers can report problems, track repairs and pay test deposits.

AI classifies requests and drafts acknowledgments for staff. Eligible repair classifications can open a fixed $75 test deposit step before staff approval. Staff approve work and assign visit times.

Next.js provides the member portal. Supabase Postgres stores member and booking data, with row-level security restricting access to each member’s records.

Edge functions validate incoming Stripe, Telegram and Airtable events. n8n processes pending outbox events to update Airtable and calls a private Supabase function to send Telegram notifications. That function records Telegram’s message ID after a successful send. Member pages read the recorded booking and payment states.

## Problem framing

Three risks shaped the implementation:

1. **Failed integrations:** A booking change may succeed while an update to Airtable or a staff notification fails.
2. **Payment confirmation:** Customers may close their browser after paying, and Stripe can resend the same event. Payment recording needs to handle both cases.
3. **AI mistakes:** A misclassified request can reach the test deposit step. Staff approval and visit times remain separate decisions, and AI drafts stay off member status pages.

## AI request triage

Claude Haiku classifies free-text requests and drafts acknowledgments for staff. The application validates the response structure, then applies a [routing policy](https://github.com/kristenmartino/cabana/blob/e9c447e029d50faaf14206a8a48cb5650f2d5e7f/lib/triage/schema.ts). Its confidence score comes from the model; it does not measure observed classification accuracy.

A repair classification with a score of at least 0.8 can open the fixed $75 Stripe test deposit step unless it is flagged as out of the service area. This happens before staff approval. The [request action](https://github.com/kristenmartino/cabana/blob/e9c447e029d50faaf14206a8a48cb5650f2d5e7f/app/request/actions.ts) routes other classifications, low scores, timeouts and invalid responses to review. Staff still approve the work and assign its visit time.

The [20-case golden suite](https://github.com/kristenmartino/cabana/blob/e9c447e029d50faaf14206a8a48cb5650f2d5e7f/tests/golden/golden.test.ts) checks expected routes and forbidden phrases in draft acknowledgments, including prompt-injection cases. It is a regression sample, rather than a general accuracy or containment result.

## Member sign-in

Registered members request an email link, open it, then select **Sign in** to confirm. The shared demo needs no email.

Tests with real emails confirmed that expired links are rejected and requesting a fresh link restores sign-in. Checks covered desktop and phone-sized browser windows, with expiry simulated on the test account.

## Member status

The dashboard shows the regular service day from the member’s plan and labels Service history entries as Completed, Cancelled or Missed. The request page separates payment receipt, staff approval and the assigned visit window.

A request that reaches scheduling without an assigned visit window shows “Awaiting visit time,” or “Approved · time pending” after approval. Payment, staff approval and a confirmed visit time are separate states.

When a visit window is recorded before approval, the app labels it “Awaiting approval” and shows the proposed time separately.

Once an approved request has a visit window, the app shows “Confirmed” with both dates, times and the Eastern time zone. The deposit receipt remains separate.

## Deposit confirmation

Cabana uses Stripe Checkout in test mode for $75 repair deposits. Payment status is updated from Stripe webhook events after their signatures are verified.

The application records event IDs so it can recognize repeated deliveries and avoid applying the same payment update twice. After Checkout, the request page displays “Confirming your payment…” until the database records the payment.

## Telegram staff approval

Staff can use **Approve** or **Needs info** on a Telegram notification. Approve records staff approval, updates the existing Airtable booking record and clears the message’s buttons. When a visit time is still unassigned, the member dashboard and request page show “Approved · time pending.” The payment receipt stays separate.

Needs info displays “Noted — reach out to the member for details.” It leaves the booking status unchanged; staff still need to contact the member.

## Visit scheduling

The owner assigns a visit window from the authorized Telegram chat using `/schedule` with the booking ID, start and end timestamps. Each timestamp must include a UTC offset or `Z`; the command does not guess a time zone.

The [scheduling function](https://github.com/kristenmartino/cabana/blob/e03157be4cf2ce42cdedeecff463f41a2e311ae3/supabase/migrations/20261024092000_visit_window_delivery.sql) saves the window and its delivery event in one transaction. It keeps the existing approval and payment states and checks for overlapping visits when a technician is assigned. Repeating the same window creates no additional event.

n8n updates the existing Airtable row with both visit bounds and sends a Telegram notification. The member app, Airtable date fields and Telegram messages display the window in `America/New_York`, including the applicable EST or EDT offset.

## Visit completion

Office staff check `mark_completed` on the Airtable booking. The [writeback handler](https://github.com/kristenmartino/cabana/blob/e03157be4cf2ce42cdedeecff463f41a2e311ae3/supabase/functions/airtable-writeback/index.ts) validates the allowed field and records the `confirmed` → `completed` transition with the actor `office:airtable`. The outbox then updates the existing Airtable row and sends the Telegram status notification.

On refresh, the request page shows “Completed” and “This request is marked completed.” The visit appears in the dashboard’s Service history. Unchecking the Airtable box does not reopen a completed visit.

## Cancelled and missed visits

Owners cancel bookings with Telegram’s `/cancel` command. Office staff enter a reason and check `mark_missed` in Airtable. This requires a confirmed visit whose recorded window has ended and saves staff identity and reason in an audit record.

Separate synthetic tests on **October 26, 2026** verified both actions across the app, existing Airtable records and Telegram. The app showed **Cancelled** or **Missed** in Service history (`cancelled` or `no_show` in the operational tools).

Future visits, unconfirmed visits and missing reasons were refused with clear feedback. Repeating the accepted missed action created no additional transition or notification.

## Visit notes

Office staff enter the completion note in Airtable’s `visit_notes` field. The writeback handler saves the text to the booking and records `applied: visit_notes` in the sync log. After the member dashboard is refreshed, Service history displays the saved note in place of the request description, as defined in the [member read layer](https://github.com/kristenmartino/cabana/blob/e03157be4cf2ce42cdedeecff463f41a2e311ae3/lib/portal/data.ts). Editing the note leaves the booking status and payment records unchanged.

## The transactional outbox

Booking status and visit-window changes are saved with their pending integration events in the same database transaction. This record of pending work is the transactional outbox.

n8n processes events to update Airtable and send notifications. Failed deliveries are retried, and deduplication keys identify repeated deliveries. Events that reach the retry limit move to a dead-letter queue for operator review.

A webhook trigger starts processing, while a 60-second sweep checks for pending events. Nightly reconciliation checks for unresolved work.

## Failure-injection testing

A recorded test used 50 bookings while the n8n consumer was stopped, Airtable authentication was interrupted and Stripe events were replayed.

All 50 booking records appeared in Airtable with no duplicates. The log also recorded 43 dead-lettered outbox events and no duplicate booking-created events or status transitions.

The test exposed a retry bug that caused repeated alerts for dead-lettered events. The sweep was updated to skip those events until an operator explicitly requeues them.

These results come from one recorded run at demo volume.

## Demo scope

The public demo uses fictional data, a shared member account and Stripe test mode. Requests and edits are saved to that account, so visitors should use sample details. Row-level security restricts the session to that member’s records.

The deposit step includes sandbox instructions; no real money is collected. The normal half-hour reset was restored after the walkthrough, so the test booking is temporary.

Booking email notifications are not implemented. The Telegram bot rejects and logs unauthorized chats; rate limiting and alerts for those attempts remain follow-up work.
