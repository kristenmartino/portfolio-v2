## What I built

Sift is a news app with AI summaries, topic search and side-by-side coverage comparisons. It also supplies background explainers, term definitions and reference pages for people, organizations and legislation mentioned in stories.

## From summaries to supporting context

I began with a categorized news feed and comparison tools, then added ways to look up the people and terms in an article. The design question was whether a reader could find relevant background without leaving the reading flow.

That led to an introductory primer, inline glossary entries and reference pages.

## Reading and comparing

The feed organizes stories into ten categories. Readers can open source reporting, save bookmarks, search topics and compare coverage from multiple outlets. Search uses a vector index, with streamed AI responses and a web-search fallback for some queries.

Reference pages bring together available information about matched politicians, organizations, bills and news outlets. Not every article entity has a page, and fields and citations vary. The curated outlet registry also does not establish which outlets are actively ingesting at a given time.

Sources include public records and named external rating providers. Outlet ratings are attributed to their providers rather than presented as Sift's own assessments. Source links help readers check claims, but their presence does not independently verify generated explanations.

## Background and live processing

The background pipeline extracts entities, links reference pages and prepares summaries and context before browsing. The frontend reads that prepared material from Postgres. Comparison and topic-search requests can require additional live AI work, so they stream results as processing proceeds.

Preparing content in advance reduces the work required when a reader opens the feed.

The companion [MCP server](https://github.com/kristenmartino/sift-mcp) exposes article search and civic profiles to AI clients. Semantic search calls Voyage before querying the database. Its organization-field gate withholds selected values when required source metadata is missing; it does not read the linked page or verify that it supports the claim. Response times depend on these database and provider calls; a current end-to-end benchmark remains unmeasured.

## Limits and next checks

Generated summaries, comparisons and entity links can be wrong or incomplete. Readers still need to check the generated interpretation against its source.

I would test whether readers can identify which source made a claim, find a relevant definition and distinguish a generated summary from original reporting. Broader claims about civic understanding or daily use would need a separate user study.

The next product work is to improve when background context appears and how easily a reader can inspect its sources.
