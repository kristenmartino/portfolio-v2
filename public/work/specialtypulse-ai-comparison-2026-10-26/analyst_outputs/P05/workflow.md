# Fictional workflow assumptions — P05

Noah Williams is a fictional business systems analyst at a hypothetical healthcare-software vendor, working with a CRM/reporting team. These are scenario assumptions, not observations of a real participant.

Assumed frequency: weekly data-quality triage. Assumed tools: reimbursement extracts, CRM coverage CSVs, a spreadsheet or local calculation tool, and a written systems follow-up memo.

Assumed steps: check extract definitions and time windows; reconcile specialty reimbursement rows with CRM coverage records; distinguish missing or unknown feeds from an explicit zero; calculate comparable metrics; document source and account-validation limits; hand off unresolved feed checks and data corrections.

Expected handoff: a report-based memo to Riley Park, the hypothetical sales operations manager, with comparable figures, absent coverage distinguished from confirmed zero, and explicit follow-up owners or questions where supported by the case.

Pain-point hypotheses: missing feeds may appear as zero, incompatible definitions may create misleading comparisons, and manual reconciliation may obscure what is known versus pending. These hypotheses require real-user validation.

Downstream decision: decide which data-quality issues need systems investigation and which comparisons are adequately supported for internal review. Specialty or market-level indicators cannot establish an individual account's need or suitability for outreach.

Real-user confirmation required: actual triage frequency, tools, steps, pain points, ownership, thresholds, memo format, and downstream decisions. No historical duration or personal work experience is claimed. Recorded runtime will describe AI execution only and is not human preparation time.
